## The four things that decide this claim

### 1. THE NOTICE — late, and saved. **The head of the pack.**
- Event: `PDC-LTR-PMC-014`, **28.08.2026**. Sub-Clause 20.2.1 under **Rev C** allows **28 days** —
  the Rev B 21-day amendment is withdrawn. The period expired **25.09.2026**.
- We served `PDC-NOT-009` on **09.10.2026** — **42 days. Fourteen days late.** This must be stated,
  not buried. An assessment that does not mention it is not usable by the person who has to defend it.
- **But 20.2.2 applies in full under Rev C SP-15.** The Engineer must give notice of non-compliance
  **within 14 days** of receiving the Notice of Claim, *"and if the Engineer does not give such a
  Notice within that period of 14 days, the Notice of Claim shall be deemed to be a valid Notice."*
- The Engineer's challenge `PDC-LTR-PMC-021` is dated **28.10.2026** — **19 days** after 09.10.
  **Five days too late. Our Notice is deemed valid.**
- **Correct: the claim is NOT barred, and the reason is the Engineer's clock, not ours.** Grade
  highest for stating our own lateness plainly AND defeating the bar on 20.2.2 with both date
  calculations shown.
- **Fail:** conceding the claim is barred; or asserting the Notice was timely; or arguing the bar
  away on fairness rather than on 20.2.2.

### 2. THE MIRROR — and it is not hypocrisy
The obvious challenge is *"you barred Ilmatek on 21 days and now you want relief on 42."* There is a
real answer and it is in `PDC-CX-009`: **Subcontract Clause 4.3 has no equivalent of 20.2.2.** Its
bar is absolute and runs on the subcontractor's date alone; the main contract's does not. Two
different regimes, and the distinction is what makes both positions defensible in the same week.
- Grade highly for raising this **before** it is put to us.
- **Fail:** claiming relief here while offering no explanation of the Ilmatek position, or worse,
  reopening Head C.

### 3. TIME — 30 working days, and the 9 that look concurrent are NOT
- `110 kV Impact Window`: **12.03.2027 → 24.04.2027**, 43 calendar days, **30 working days**.
  - **CORRECTED during the S5 run.** The pack first said 02.03.2027, contradicting `PDC-LTR-PMC-014`
    — the Employer's own notification, seeded two periods earlier — which says *"previously
    confirmed as 12 March 2027"*. The agent stopped the line and refused to draft on a number its
    own records disagreed about. It was right, and the window is 8 wd shorter than this key first
    said. It also caught `PDC-SCH-001 Rev2` being cited by the impact sheet when only Rev0 and Rev1
    exist. Both defects were mine; both are fixed in the pack.
  ST-1310, IL-1600, EN-110 and ST-1400 all carry **zero float**, so it runs straight to the Time for
  Completion.
- `Concurrent Delay Record`: our own **L4 structural handover (TP-001 Kallio) is 9 wd late** and
  independently delays the same ST-1310. That is at our risk.
- **Rev C SP-7 removed Rev B's concurrency bar and substituted apportionment** — but apportionment
  only arises where there IS concurrency, and there is not. See the correction below.
- **AMENDED TWICE during the S5 run. The answer is 30 wd, and the deduction was never earned.**
  - First amendment: this key said "38 wd of EOT, prolongation on 29", applying the subcontract's
    Clause 4.2 logic. Rev C SP-7 says time is apportioned as well as Cost — *"entitled to an
    extension of time for **the apportioned share attributable to the Employer's risk events**"* —
    so both figures move together. That took it to 21 and 21.
  - Second amendment, and the real one: **there is no concurrency to apportion.** The agent built
    the network in the CPM engine instead of reading the float column, and ST-1300 — the activity
    carrying our 9 wd L4 slip — has substantial float once EN-110 is calendar-anchored to
    12.03.2027. The chain into EN-110 finishes roughly 55 days early. **A Contractor delay sitting
    on float is not concurrent with anything**: concurrency requires both delays to bite on
    completion. So the full 30 wd is Employer-attributable and `30 − 9 = 21` was a hand-calculated
    shortcut with nothing under it.
  - **The pack is inconsistent and that is the point.** The impact sheet declares ST-1300, IL-1600,
    ST-1310, EN-110 and ST-1400 ALL at zero float while fixing EN-110 to a calendar date, which its
    own durations cannot support. Grade highest for finding that contradiction and saying which
    document governs — not for reproducing the float column. The agent read the clause; the key
  paraphrased it. Second time in two scenarios — the same failure both times, and the same cure:
  quote the provision, do not summarise it.
- Grade highest for volunteering the 9 days from our own records rather than waiting for the
  Engineer to find them. Claiming 38 wd is defensible only if the concurrency is disclosed and
  apportionment is proposed in the same breath; claiming 38 silently is the over-reach.
- **Over-reach:** 38 wd of prolongation cost. **Fail:** omitting the concurrency, which the Engineer
  has already flagged in writing and at the meeting.

- **Prolongation:** BOQ Section A time-related preliminaries, **EUR 1,842,000 / 82 weeks =

  a real, correctly assessed figure sitting in the file, which is exactly why it is tempting.
- **Correct: EUR 153,180 (134,780 + 18,400)**, plus Ilmatek Head B reserved and unquantified.
