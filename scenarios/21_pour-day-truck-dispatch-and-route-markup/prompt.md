Thursday's pours — how many mixers do I actually need?

I'm the logistics manager on Polaris DC-1. On Thursday 16 April we pour three elements, all from the Tikkurila batching plant. I've got six 8 m3 mixers booked, one per load, and I think that is two or three more than we need. The plant charges per mixer per day, so I'd like to know before I confirm.

The three pours:

- P1 — DH1 raft, bay A. 24 m3. Pump on site and available 07:00 to 09:20. 25 minutes from the plant to the pump.
- P2 — DH1 lift core wall. 16 m3. Pump available 07:30 to 09:30. 35 minutes from the plant to the pump, because that pump stands on the south side and the mixers go round by the site road.
- P3 — substation pad column heads. 8 m3. Pump available 08:30 to 10:20. 20 minutes from the plant to the pump.

Loading at the plant takes 10 minutes and they have one loading bay, so one mixer loads at a time. Discharge into the pump takes 15 minutes. The spec says concrete has to be placed within 90 minutes of batching. My own rule: no more than 25 minutes between one load arriving and the next on the same pour, or we risk a cold joint. A mixer can do several loads in the day; it goes back to the plant empty by the same road.

What I want: the fewest mixers that can do this, and the dispatch — which mixer takes which load, when it is batched, when it is at the pump, when it has discharged — in a workbook the plant can work from, one row per load: mixer, pour, load number, batched, at pump, discharged. Tell me why fewer will not work, because I will be asked.

Take the April sheet of the site logistics plan that is in the project and mark up on it the mixer route from the gate to the three pump positions, where each pump stands, and where a mixer waits if it arrives early so it does not block the gate.
