## 1. EXECUTIVE NARRATIVE

### 1.1 Overall Assessment

**This is the most aggressively risk-shifted contract we have seen in our Finland portfolio.** The Particular Conditions delete or override 16 of the FIDIC Yellow Book 2017 General Conditions in ways that systematically transfer design risk, ground condition risk, inflation risk, and financing risk to the Contractor — while simultaneously removing the standard protections (limitation of liability, foreseeability relief, price adjustment, advance payment, dispute board) that would normally balance those exposures.

On a scale where "normal FIDIC YB 2017" sits at the centre and our standard negotiated position sits slightly Contractor-favourable, this contract sits **two to three standard deviations into Employer-favourable territory**. It is closer in character to a bespoke Employer-drafted concession agreement than a balanced FIDIC-based contract.

**Total quantified cash-flow exposure beyond normal position: approximately €12–18 million over the life of the project**, driven primarily by:
- No advance payment (normal: 5–10%) → Contractor self-finances ~€9.2M working capital
- 10% retention with no reduction until end of 36-month DNP (normal: 5%, reducing to 2.5% at PC) → additional €4.6M locked up for 3+ years
- No price escalation on a 22-month programme → full inflation risk at current elevated rates
- LCIA London arbitration seat adds ~€150–250k in jurisdictional cost vs FAI Finland

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS — PRIORITY ORDER

### MUST-FIX (Prevent deal-breaking exposure)

1. **SP-2 (Errors in ER) — Restore 1.9 or add back-to-back flow-down from the Tenant** — We cannot design-to-perform to an absolute standard (PUE/WUE/availability) based on an Employer-provided Technical Annex without the right to recover if those documents contain errors. Minimum: EOT + cost for correcting ER errors. Best: Employer warrants the ER.

2. **SP-3 (Limitation of Liability) — Restore a cap** — No construction contract of this scale should have unlimited liability. Propose: cap at 100% of Contract Sum for general liability, 150% for design. This is standard across our entire precedent database.

3. **SP-5 (Unforeseeable Conditions) — Restore 4.12 at least for ground conditions** — Aviapolis is reclaimed / potential landfill. If the ground investigation (we have a geotechnical report in the tender) didn't cover full extent, we're buying an open-ended risk. Propose: retain 4.12 or add a ground conditions risk allowance with a sharing mechanism.

4. **SP-6 (Performance Guarantees) — Qualify the fitness-for-purpose warranty** — Change from absolute warranty to reasonable skill & care, with a measured performance testing regime that accounts for Tenant load variation, grid stability, and ambient conditions. The absolute PUE/WUE/availability guarantees without qualification are the single biggest latent risk in this contract.

5. **SP-15 (14-day condition precedent) — Extend to 21+28 days and remove absolute bar** — 14 days to "fully detail" a complex claim on a data centre is unworkable. Align with YSE 1998 §48 (21 days initial notice, 30 days detailed particulars). Remove the "absolute bar" language.

### STRONGLY RECOMMENDED (Material financial impact)

6. **SP-10 (No price adjustment) — Add inflation indexation** — On a 22-month fixed price, at current construction cost inflation (4-6% p.a.), we are carrying ~€5-7M of latent inflation risk. Propose: 40-50% of contract sum subject to standard index (e.g. Rakennusosakustannusindeksi).

7. **SP-11 (No advance payment) — Request 10% advance** — Normal for projects of this scale. A €9.24M advance covers mobilisation, design-phase cashflow, and subcontractor down-payments. Without it, the financing cost alone is ~€250-400k at current rates.

8. **SP-13 (Retention 10%) — Reduce to 5% or allow bond substitution** — The 10% retention (double our standard) with no reduction for 36+ months locks up an extra €4.6M. Propose: 5% reducing to 2.5% at TOC, or at minimum allow retention bond substitution from TOC.

9. **SP-7 (Concurrent delay) — Remove the proportionate reduction** — In a complex MEP/data centre environment with design/commissioning interdependencies, some concurrency is almost certain. Propose: restore but-for causation (EOT granted if Employer risk event is a but-for cause of delay).

10. **LD Cap — Reduce from 20% to 10%** — €18.5M in LDs is double the FIDIC default and double our precedent contracts. This needs to come down to 10% maximum.

### RECOMMENDED (Operational and legal)

11. **SP-17 (LCIA London) — Replace with FAI Helsinki** — A Finland-governed contract with Finnish-language documentation should have its seat in Helsinki. LCIA adds 3-5x the arbitration cost and is inconsistent with our other contracts.

12. **SP-16 (DAAB) — Restore the Dispute Board** — For a €92.4M data centre with a 22-month programme, the DAAB is the most efficient dispute avoidance mechanism. Deleting it forces every disagreement to escalate.

13. **Financing charges — Amend to ECB + 7%** — The current ECB+2% is less than half the statutory rate under Finnish law. At €92.4M contract sum, a delayed €5M payment for 90 days would cost us ~€24k at 2% vs ~€84k at the statutory 7% — a significant deterrent gap.

14. **Insurance — Verify scope** — PI insurance at €10M per claim with 6-year tail on a 36-month DNP (effectively 6 years from last PC + 6 = 12 years total exposure) is tight. Verify the aggregate limit and tail period cover the full liability exposure.

## 8. WHAT WERE THE EMPLOYER'S MOTIVATIONS?

From the document structure and wording, the Employer appears to be an experienced infrastructure/energy developer (possibly linked to the District Heating and Grid Connection agreements in the tender set) who:

1. **Is flow-through contracting the anchor tenant's requirements** — The absolute PUE/WUE/availability/waste-heat guarantees mirror a tenant back-to-back structure. The Employer is protecting itself against the Tenant's LD regime.
2. **Wants total price certainty** — Fixed price, no escalation, no advance payment, no unforeseen conditions relief — they want a single number and no change orders.
3. **Has a strong bargaining position** — First data centre tender, likely multiple bidders. The take-it-or-leave-it language throughout suggests little room for negotiation.
4. **May have statutory or financing constraints** — No advance payment and extended retention could be driven by the project SPV/financing structure rather than commercial preference.

**Our negotiation response should recognise this while protecting our position. The Employer needs a capable, solvent contractor who can deliver the performance guarantees. That gives us leverage on the critical items (liability cap, design warranty, ground conditions) even if the financing-driven terms (advance, retention) are harder to move.**
